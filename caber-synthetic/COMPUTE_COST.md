# Compute cost — 3D TDEM forward modelling

All timings for the dipping-target synthetic. Measured on the machine described at the bottom; every number here comes from a JSON written by a script in this directory, and this file is regenerated by `_build_compute_report.py`.

**The three things worth remembering:**

1. A forward is **4 factorizations and 80 solve calls** — the factorization count is set by the number of *unique time-step sizes*, and the solve-call count by the number of *time steps*. Neither depends on how many sources you have.
2. On the global mesh a single factorization costs about as much as **110 solves**, so the factorization dominates a single-sounding forward (76% of it).
3. Adding sources does **not** add solves or factorizations — they ride along as extra right-hand-side columns. What does scale linearly with source count is the **RHS assembly**, and past ~10 sources that becomes the dominant cost. See §5.

---

## 1. The problem being solved

- **Global mesh:** 52,340 cells (42,284 active), **147,204 edges** — the E-field system is 147,204 × 147,204.
- **Time stepping:** (3e-06 s × 20), (1e-05 s × 20), (3e-05 s × 20), (0.0001 s × 20) = **80 steps**, **4 unique step sizes**.
- **Physics / solver:** `Simulation3DElectricField` (backward Euler), pymatsolver Pardiso (MKL) direct solver.
- **Surveys:** 80 m station spacing → 10 × 5 lines = **50 soundings** (the 3D inversions); 40 m spacing → 20 × 5 = **100 soundings** (stitched-1D, and the 100-source forward in §6).

A factorization is reused for as long as `dt` is constant, so the **4 unique step sizes give 4 factorizations**, each shared by the 20 steps in its block.

## 2. Where the time goes — one sounding, one thread

The reference case. Median of 3 warm runs; instrumented by `_timing_breakdown.py`, which subclasses the Pardiso solver to time each factorization and each solve, and the simulation to time each assembly.

| stage | calls | total (s) | per call | share |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| factorization | 4 | 71.80 | 18.0 s | 75.7% |
| solve | 80 | 13.08 | 164 ms | 13.8% |
| RHS assembly (`getRHS`) | 80 | 9.02 | 113 ms | 9.5% |
| system matrix (`getAdiag`) | 4 | 0.21 | 52 ms | 0.2% |
| sub-diagonal (`getAsubdiag`) | 80 | 0.04 | 0.5 ms | 0.0% |
| other / field storage | — | 0.73 | — | 0.8% |
| **`fields()` total** | | **94.88** | | |
| receiver interpolation | | 0.43 | | |
| **forward total** | | **95.31** | | |

This reproduces the independent measurement in `_forward_timing.md` (95.3 s here vs 96.0 s there).

The **cold** first call costs **117.4 s** (+23 s), because it also builds discretize's mesh operators — the curl and the edge/face inner-product matrices — which are then cached on the mesh. Pay it once per session, not per forward.

## 3. Per time-step block

One sounding, one thread. One factorization per block, reused by every step in that block.

| block | dt (s) | steps | t range (s) | factorization (s) | solve/step (s) | block solves (s) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 3e-06 | 20 | 0.00e+00 – 6.00e-05 | 17.95 | 0.164 | 3.27 |
| 2 | 1e-05 | 20 | 6.00e-05 – 2.60e-04 | 18.06 | 0.164 | 3.27 |
| 3 | 3e-05 | 20 | 2.60e-04 – 8.60e-04 | 17.85 | 0.163 | 3.26 |
| 4 | 0.0001 | 20 | 8.60e-04 – 2.86e-03 | 17.94 | 0.164 | 3.28 |

Every factorization costs the same to within 1% (17.9–18.1 s): the sparsity pattern is identical across blocks, only the `dt` scaling of the mass term changes. Solves are equally flat at 154–169 ms.

> **One factorization (18.0 s) ≈ 110 solves (164 ms each).**

## 4. Threading

| threads | sources | `fields()` (s) | factorization (s) | solves (s) | assembly (s) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 1 | 94.9 | 71.8 | 13.1 | 9.3 |
| 24 | 1 | 28.2 | 12.0 | 5.1 | 10.3 |
| 24 | 10 | 125.0 | 12.0 | 19.0 | 88.9 |
| 24 | 50 | 529.7 | 12.1 | 29.4 | 456.1 |

24 threads speeds a single-sounding forward up **3.4×** (95 → 28 s). The gain is entirely inside MKL — factorization 6.0×, solves 2.6× — while **assembly does not thread at all** (9.3 → 10.3 s; it is scipy sparse work on one core). Assembly therefore rises from 10% to 37% of the runtime.

## 5. How cost scales with the number of sources

**Sources do not multiply the solve count.** SimPEG solves every source simultaneously at each time step: the right-hand side is a matrix of shape `(n_edges, n_src)`, not a vector, and the time-stepping loop runs over time steps only.

```python
for tInd, dt in enumerate(self.time_steps):        # 80 iterations
    sol = Ainv * (rhs - Asubdiag * f[..., tInd])   # rhs is (n_edges, n_src)
```

So for **any** source count: **4 factorizations, 80 solve calls**. 100 sources means 80 calls each carrying 100 right-hand sides (8,000 back-substitutions in total), but they are batched, and batching is far cheaper than solving one at a time. Measured:

| sources | threads | factorization calls | solve calls | solves (s) | per solve call (ms) | RHS assembly (s) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 1 | 4 | 80 | 13.1 | 164 | 9.0 |
| 1 | 24 | 4 | 80 | 5.1 | 63 | 10.1 |
| 10 | 24 | 4 | 80 | 19.0 | 238 | 88.7 |
| 50 | 24 | 4 | 80 | 29.4 | 368 | 455.8 |

Going from 1 to 50 sources multiplies the number of right-hand sides by 50× but the solve time by only **5.8×** — the triangular back-substitution amortises across columns.

**RHS assembly is the term that scales linearly** — about **9.1 s per source** across the measured range. `getRHS` re-discretises the source term onto the mesh at every step, and with a `StepOffWaveform` the transmitter is off for `t > 0`: the returned vector is **exactly zero for steps 2–80** (verified directly). 79 of the 80 evaluations are wasted work.

| sources | share of `fields()` spent in RHS assembly |
|---:|---:|
| 1 | 36% |
| 10 | 71% |
| 50 | 86% |

Extrapolating 9.1 s/source to the 100-source global forward in §6 predicts **~912 s** of RHS assembly out of its 1501 s measured total — i.e. most of that run was source-term rebuilding, not linear algebra.

> This is a SimPEG-side inefficiency, not a property of the problem: caching the source term across the steps of a step-off waveform would remove most of it. Worth knowing before quoting the global multi-source wall time as the cost of the method.

## 6. Tiled vs. global mesh

### One sounding (`_forward_timing.md`)

| quantity | tiled (local mesh) | global mesh | ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| mesh cells | 5,510 | 52,340 | 9.5× |
| active cells | 3,602 | 42,284 | 11.7× |
| `dpred` wall time (median, s) | 2.71 | 96.04 | **35.5× slower** |
| peak RSS (MB) | 663 | 1699 | 2.6× |
| `dpred` RAM increment (MB) | 61 | 1110 | 18.2× |

### 100 soundings (`_ram_usage.md`)

| quantity | tiled | global | ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| processes | 48 | 1 | |
| `dpred` wall time (s) | 32.4 | 1500.8 | **46× slower** |
| peak RSS (GB) | 13.2 | 11.0 | 0.84× |

**Note the memory row.** The tiling win is *time*, not memory: at 100 soundings the tiled run is 46× faster but its peak RSS (13.2 GB) is comparable to — in fact slightly above — the global run's (11.0 GB), because 48 worker processes each hold their own local mesh and fields. Do not claim tiling uses less memory overall; per *forward solve* it does (18× less, §6 above), but the process-level total does not shrink.

## Machine / software

- **CPU:** Intel(R) Xeon(R) CPU E5-2670 v3 @ 2.30GHz — 2 sockets × 12 cores, 48 logical
- **RAM:** 126 GB
- **Linear solver:** pymatsolver Pardiso (MKL). The instrumentation constructs it with `factor=False` and then calls `_factor()` explicitly, so the factorization is separated from the first solve rather than hidden inside it (verified not to change the result: residual ~1e-14).
- Python 3.11.11, SimPEG 0.24.1.dev33+g42a5db944, discretize 0.11.3, pymatsolver 0.3.1, numpy 2.1.3, scipy 1.15.1
